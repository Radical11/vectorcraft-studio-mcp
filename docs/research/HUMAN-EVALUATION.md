# Human evaluation protocol

Status: prepared, not conducted. Owner blind feedback is a development/release preference; it is not a professional panel or population study. No participant responses or comprehension results are invented.

## Owner pilot comparison

After all builds are accounted for, show twelve anonymous paired comparisons: six complete-process/default pairs and six complete-process/blockout pairs. Balance left/right positions and randomize pair order using the frozen map. Supply the brief and equal full/use-size images, without creator scores, condition, target or history. The owner may select A, B, equal, or cannot assess, explain visible reasons, and flag a consequential regression. Owner input cannot retune or rebuild a completed pilot trial.

Anonymous labels do not guarantee successful blinding. The owner has seen development progress and may infer a condition from style or availability. Record prior exposure and any inferred identity after preference collection; disclose this limitation without using it to discard inconvenient responses. Missing artwork remains visible as missing, with no substituted calibration or unconfirmed final image.

Keep communication, drawing/typographic craft, cohesion, and overall brief preference separate. A different style or more detail alone is not improvement. A consequential regression means a new meaningful loss relative to the other result—wrong required content, confused action/association, unreadable essential copy, or production/editability failure—not a numeric score change or a weakness shared by both.

## Later designer and reader study

Prepare recruitment only after pilot variability/failure modes are known. Distinguish sustained design practice from general readers, select participant and brief counts based on the target effect and a power/precision analysis, and obtain any required institutional review before collection. Participation is voluntary; provide purpose, task/time estimate, confidentiality and withdrawal terms, and separate permission for publishing comments. Do not collect identifiers unnecessarily.

Designers assess anchored brief preference and local consequential defects at equal intended sizes, with optional full-size examination recorded. Readers perform short brief-specific recognition/comprehension tasks before preference questions. For the library graphic, ask which action follows On loan and which branch reaches a librarian. For the illustration, ask what the working and supporting hands are doing. For the poster, ask what the activity is, when/where it occurs and required preparation. Do not infer comprehension from model answers.

For reader tasks, show the artwork and only neutral viewing instructions before collecting recognition/comprehension answers. Withhold the design brief, intended interpretation and answer-bearing questions or labels until those answers are recorded. Then supply the common brief for separate suitability/preference questions. A question must not disclose its own expected answer. Prespecify the answer key and distinguish spontaneous recognition from prompted comprehension; the owner brief-based comparison above is a different task.

Counterbalance presentation; avoid repeated exposure that teaches the answer. Include order-swap reliability checks on a prespecified subset, record disagreement and confidence/cannot-assess responses, and keep relevant experience separate from performance. Analyze clustering by brief and participant. Assess model/human agreement per dimension, not just overall majority preference. Report exclusions, missingness and deviations before inspecting conclusions.

This later gate supports claims about human communication and professional preference. It does not automatically establish acceptance at a specific venue or universal professional superiority.
