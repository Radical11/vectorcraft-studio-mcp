# Design assessment

Apply the brief's audience, style, use size and constraints. A logo, editorial poster, diagram and illustration have different needs. Do not force typography onto a wordless symbol or a rigid grid onto expressive artwork.

| Key | Criterion | Inspect |
|---|---|---|
| brief | Communication and brief | Required content, purpose, audience, message and accurate information |
| composition | Composition and hierarchy | Focal point, reading sequence, scale, balance and negative space |
| alignment | Alignment and spacing | Grid relationships, margins, repeated gaps, optical versus geometric alignment |
| typography | Typography | Legibility at use size, pairing, weight/size hierarchy, wrapping, kerning and line spacing |
| color | Color and contrast | Cohesive palette, readable foreground/background, relevant monochrome or background variants |
| style | Art direction | Consistency of geometry, strokes, visual language, texture, shadows and effects |
| distinctiveness | Distinctiveness | Specific, intentional concept rather than interchangeable decoration or a copied reference |
| craft | Craft and usability | Unintended clipping/overlaps, path quality, scalability, usable exports and editability |

Score anchors: **1** unusable or contradicts the brief; **2** major defects; **3** functional but unfinished, generic, or visually weaker than the agreed direction; **4** resolved for this audience/medium, with purposeful subject-specific imagery, copy and craft and only minor issues; **5** unusually resolved original decisions and execution. Neat alignment and readable type alone do not earn 4 for composition, style or distinctiveness. Scores are subjective judgments, not credentials. Use null with a reason for a genuinely inapplicable criterion.

For every critical/major/minor finding, identify the visual region or object, observed evidence, consequence for the audience and a concrete correction. Label preference-level comments separately. Critical means the result cannot serve its required purpose; major means a significant obstacle to communication or usability; minor is local refinement. Do not inflate severity to justify another round.

Use the native inspection for numerical relationships, the full render for composition, and intended-size render for legibility. Quantities support a finding rather than replace visual judgment. Flag intentional bleed, masked content and optical adjustments accurately. Do not claim WCAG compliance from a flat artwork preview, or invent a numeric contrast ratio without calculation and valid colors.

When comparing candidate and incumbent, judge both against the same brief. Choose candidate only for a clear net improvement; prefer incumbent on regressions, and equal when the difference is only taste. Explain the tradeoff. Do not assume the latest version is superior.

References informing spacing/type consistency (not templates to copy): https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/spacing/ and https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale/ .

For v3, also score visual_fidelity (1..5) against observable target features and copy_assessment (1..5 or null for wordless art). Assess silhouette, atmosphere, spatial relationships, meaningful detail and intentional departures; do not demand pixel duplication. Copy should belong to the subject and audience, not interchangeable slogans. Locked user text is a constraint, not a reason to penalize a designer for not rewriting it. A pass requires all applicable scores >=4 and no major/critical findings. A critic can pass a resolved design with no changes; never invent defects to force iteration.
